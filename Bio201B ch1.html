Positive vs. negative controls — the logic

Positive control contains the variable being tested for. It reacts positively and proves the test can actually detect what you expect — it shows you what a true positive looks like.
Negative control contains only the solvent (usually distilled water, no solute). It shows what a true negative looks like, so any color change elsewhere can be trusted.

Background concepts

Lipids are a diverse group of hydrophobic molecules. The unifying feature of a lipid is little or no affinity for water — lipids are water-fearing. The three most biologically important types of lipid are fats, phospholipids, and steroids.
DNA structure — DNA is usually double-stranded, with the two strands held together by hydrogen bonds between nitrogen bases. That double strand is twisted into a double helix.
